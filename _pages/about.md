# About

I am a postdoctoral researcher at the [Simons Institute](https://simons.berkeley.edu/) at UC Berkeley, hosted by [Peter Bartlett](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~bartlett/) and [Bin Yu](https://binyu.stat.berkeley.edu/), and affiliated with the [Machine Learning Research Pod](https://simons.berkeley.edu/research-pods/machine-learning-research-pod) and the [Berkeley Statistical Machine Learning Group](https://statml.berkeley.edu/). I received my PhD in [Statistics and Data Science](https://statistics.yale.edu/) from Yale University in 2025, advised by [Andrew Barron](http://www.stat.yale.edu/~arb4/), and my undergraduate and master's degrees in [Applied Mathematics and Engineering](https://www.queensu.ca/mathstat/undergraduate/prospective-undergraduate/mthe) from Queen's University, advised by [Serdar Yüksel](https://serdar-yuksel.github.io/).

The main theme of my research is the convergence behaviour of stochastic processes: how error propagates through a random system, how quickly a system converges to its invariant distribution, and the properties of this distribution in downstream applications. Recently I have focused on sampling from multimodal and non-log-concave distributions, where traditional MCMC methods based on time invariant local likelihood updates can have difficulty exploring the full state space. Time varying transition rules, annealing, auxiliary random variables, and diffusion models all present interesting alternatives to produce samples for such densities beyond traditional MCMC.  Earlier work addressed filter stability in hidden Markov models and its applications to robust stochastic control. I am also interested in applications of generative models in the physical sciences and to AI safety.

I co-organize the [ML and AI Theory Seminar](https://simons.berkeley.edu/events/ml-ai-theory-seminar-0) and reading group.

I co-founded and facilitate the [Mathematical Foundations of Diffusion Models](https://cmcdonald-1.github.io/diffusion-models-reading-group/) reading group, a series of student run introductory lectures for graduate students new to the area.

For more information, please see my [Publications](https://cmcdonald-1.github.io/publications/) page, [CV](https://cmcdonald-1.github.io/assets/pdf/CV_2026_academic_v4a.pdf), or [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/curtis-mcdonald-010a63254/) profile.
